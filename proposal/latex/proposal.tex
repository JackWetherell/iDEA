\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Kohn's principle of nearsightedness says that local electronic properties depend only weakly on distant changes in the external potential. It underlies linear-scaling methods, embedding theories and every local or semilocal approximation in electronic-structure theory. In earlier work with the \iDEA{} code~\cite{wetherell2019}, we showed that the self-energy $\Sigma$ of many-body perturbation theory (MBPT) is advantageously nearsighted, whereas the exact Kohn--Sham (KS) potential is not. That study used \emph{approximate} self-energies, spinless electrons and weakly correlated systems.

The decisive test is strong coupling: stretched bonds, Mott insulators and Wigner molecules. This is where both density-functional theory (DFT) and \GW{} fail, and where few-electron Wigner physics is now imaged in nanotubes, quantum dots and moir\'e artificial atoms~\cite{pecker2013,shapir2019,corrigan2021,li2024}.

We propose to compute, for the first time, \emph{exact} one-body Green's functions and self-energies of interacting electrons in the one-dimensional continuum across the crossover into the Wigner regime, and to compare them with \GW{} and with exact KS potentials.

\textbf{Central hypothesis:} whether the exact self-energy is nearsighted is controlled by near-degeneracy and spin.
\begin{itemize}
\item It remains nearsighted for non-degenerate subsystems and for same-spin electrons.
\item In the strongly correlated singlet regime it develops a low-frequency component that couples distant subsystems, with separation-independent weight: the real-space fingerprint of the Mott pole.
\item \GW{} remains nearsighted throughout and misses it.
\end{itemize}

A closed-form result for the Hubbard dimer, verified numerically, already shows this structure. The project uses \iDEA's GPU-accelerated exact solver, the space-time \GW{} implementation in open pull request \#3, and a new exact-Green's-function module. It targets \emph{Phys.\ Rev.\ B}, or \emph{Phys.\ Rev.\ Lett.} if the breakdown proves clean and general. Planned duration is about six months part-time, with under 50 GPU-hours.
\end{abstract}

\section{Background and motivation}

\subsection{Nearsightedness, and what we showed in 2019}
Kohn introduced nearsightedness to justify linear-scaling DFT~\cite{kohn1996}. Prodan and Kohn sharpened it: at fixed chemical potential, the density at a point responds only to nearby changes in the effective potential~\cite{prodan2005}.

The exact KS potential is \emph{not} nearsighted in this sense:
\begin{itemize}
\item For weakly coupled subsystems with different local ionisation energies, the exact exchange--correlation (xc) potential forms spatial steps whose height is set globally~\cite{kraisler2021,hodgson2016,hodgson2017}.
\item In the symmetric stretched singlet, it forms a peak at the bond midpoint~\cite{buijse1989,helbig2009}.
\end{itemize}

In Ref.~\cite{wetherell2019}, two electrons were confined to separate one-dimensional (1D) wells. The exact KS potential showed a step, whereas Hartree--Fock and \GW{} self-energies did not. When distant subsystems were combined, the self-energy demonstrated nearsightedness numerically. Related nonlocal-potential schemes point the same way: Koopmans-compliant hybrids~\cite{elmaslmane2018} and generalised KS with a local correlation potential~\cite{hodgson2020}.

\subsection{Why strong coupling is the decisive test}
The 2019 conclusion is only as strong as \GW's physics, and \GW{} fails qualitatively in strongly correlated regimes:
\begin{itemize}
\item static correlation and delocalisation errors in H$_2$ dissociation~\cite{caruso2013,hellgren2015};
\item the missing vertex in the atomic (Mott) limit of the Hubbard dimer~\cite{romaniello2009};
\item self-consistency making results worse for two electrons on a sphere~\cite{loos2018}.
\end{itemize}

In this regime the exact self-energy is expected to contain a Mott pole near the chemical potential, exactly the kind of feature that might \emph{not} be nearsighted. The answer matters for:
\begin{itemize}
\item local and embedding self-energy schemes, such as dynamical mean-field theory with its local self-energy~\cite{georges1996};
\item connector theory, which transfers spectral information from models to materials~\cite{gatti2007,vanzini2022,vanzini2017,vanzini2018};
\item practical ``nearsighted'' MBPT-inspired functionals.
\end{itemize}

Strongly correlated few-electron systems are also experimentally topical~\cite{pecker2013,shapir2019,corrigan2021,li2024}, and are the subject of recent \emph{ab initio} 1D studies~\cite{lagasco2026,lagasco2025}.

\subsection{What is missing}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Lattice only.} Exact self-energies exist only for lattice models: the Hubbard dimer~\cite{romaniello2009}, its spectral potential~\cite{vanzini2018}, and exact \GW{} and vertex benchmarks in the extended Hubbard model~\cite{exthubbard2026}. A recent analysis argues that the inferred cancellation of vertex corrections needs re-examination~\cite{forster2024}.
\item \textbf{No real-space map.} Nobody has mapped the real-space nonlocality of an exact self-energy against separation and correlation strength, side by side with \GW{} and the exact KS potential.
\item \textbf{Local frequency-dependent alternatives untested.} Neither spectral potentials~\cite{gatti2007,vanzini2017} nor dynamical xc fields~\cite{aryasetiawan2025} have been tested against exact continuum references.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why now, and why \iDEA}
\iDEA{} (v1.1.0) solves the many-electron Schr\"odinger equation exactly on a 1D grid, for any potential, interaction and spin configuration, with a GPU-accelerated solver. It also provides mean-field methods, an experimental KS strictly-correlated-electrons (KS-SCE) module~\cite{malet2012,malet2013,marie2022} and exact KS inversion. Three developments make this project feasible now:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Space-time \GW.} Open PR~\#3 adds $G_0W_0$, $GW_0$ and fully self-consistent \GW{} in the space-time formalism~\cite{rojas1995,rieger1999}. It uses optimised imaginary-time and imaginary-frequency grids in the spirit of the discrete Lehmann representation~\cite{kaye2022}, and Galitskii--Migdal energies~\cite{galitskii1958}.
\item \textbf{GPU acceleration.} Three-electron Hamiltonians on grids of about 100--150 points are now practical. This is exactly the $(N+1)$ sector needed for electron addition to a two-electron system.
\item \textbf{Consolidated fixes.} A set of fixes and many-body observables is ready on feature branches (Sec.~\ref{sec:prereq}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Central hypothesis and preliminary analytic result}\label{sec:dimer}

\subsection{The Hubbard dimer}
For the half-filled Hubbard dimer, $H=-t\sum_\sigma(c^\dagger_{1\sigma}c_{2\sigma}+\mathrm{h.c.})+U\sum_i n_{i\uparrow}n_{i\downarrow}$, the exact self-energy relative to the non-interacting Hamiltonian is diagonal in the bonding/antibonding basis~\cite{romaniello2009}:
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{b/a}(\omega)=\frac{U}{2}+\frac{U^2/4}{\omega-\mu\mp 3t},\qquad \mu=\frac{U}{2}.
\end{equation}
In the site basis, at imaginary frequency $\omega=\mu+i\nu$,
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{12}(\mu+i\nu)=-\frac{3tU^2/4}{\nu^2+9t^2},\qquad
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!d\nu\,\Sigma_{12}(\mu+i\nu)=-\frac{\pi U^2}{4},\qquad
\Sigma_{12}(\mu)=-\frac{U^2}{12t}.
\label{eq:dimer}
\end{equation}
We verified these expressions by exact diagonalisation in Fock space (Appendix~\ref{app:dimer}). The pole position, residue and constant are reproduced to ten digits for $0.05\le t/U\le0.2$. The weight $-\pi U^2/4$ is reproduced to quadrature accuracy for $0.01\le t/U\le0.2$.

\subsection{Interpretation}
As the sites separate, $t$ decays exponentially and the inter-site self-energy behaves in three ways at once:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Finite frequency:} at fixed $\nu\gg t$, $\Sigma_{12}\approx-3tU^2/(4\nu^2)\propto t$, so it is \emph{nearsighted}.
\item \textbf{Chemical potential:} $\Sigma_{12}(\mu)\propto 1/t$ grows, so it is \emph{ultra-far-sighted}.
\item \textbf{Integrated weight:} independent of separation.
\end{itemize}
The nonlocal part does not disappear. It collapses onto a window of width $\sim3t$ around $\mu$: the Mott pole viewed in real space.

\subsection{Two controls that restore nearsightedness}
\textbf{Asymmetry.} Site energies $\pm\Delta/2$ suppress the inter-site self-energy, but only gradually (Table~\ref{tab:asym}).

\textbf{Spin.} For two same-spin electrons on two sites, the ground state is a single determinant and $\Sigma$ reduces to its static Hartree--Fock form. There is no low-frequency nonlocal component.

\subsection{What the continuum adds}
The dimer cannot tell us four things:
\begin{itemize}
\item the real-space range and shape of the nonlocal component;
\item the role of long-range Coulomb tails and of the internal structure of Wigner molecules;
\item the quantitative comparison with the exact KS potential (steps and peaks);
\item whether \GW{} captures any of it.
\end{itemize}

\section{Research questions and hypotheses}

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1em,labelindent=0pt,itemsep=6pt]
\item[RQ1 (exact $\Sigma$ in the 2019 setting).] Question: in an asymmetric double well with one electron per well, is the exact $\Sigma(x,x';i\nu)$ nearsighted, for both singlet and triplet?\\
\textit{H1:} for asymmetry $\Delta\gg\teff$, the exact $\Sigma$ is nearsighted at all frequencies while the exact $\vxc$ shows the step; singlet and triplet behave alike.\\
\textit{Falsified by:} a sizeable inter-well block at low $\nu$.

\item[RQ2 (symmetric dissociation; central).] Question: how does the inter-subsystem block $\Sigma_{AB}(i\nu)$ scale with separation $d$ and frequency, and does it collapse onto Eq.~\eqref{eq:dimer} with $\teff(d)$ and $\Ueff$ extracted from the exact spectra?\\
\textit{H2:} in the singlet,
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Vert\Sigma_{AB}\Vert$ at fixed $\nu$ decays like $\teff(d)$;
\item $\Sigma_{AB}(\mu)$ grows like $1/\teff$;
\item the integrated weight is $d$-independent, $\approx\pi\Ueff^2/4$ on localised orbitals.
\end{itemize}
The triplet shows no such component, and asymmetry restores nearsightedness for $\Delta$ between $\sim\teff$ and $\Ueff$.\\
\textit{Falsified by:} a weight that decays with $d$, or a triplet with the same low-frequency component.

\item[RQ3 (Wigner molecule in a single soft well).] Question: does the spatial range of $\Sigma$ track the Wigner spacing as the coupling $\lambda$ grows, and at which frequencies?\\
\textit{H3 (exploratory):} the range grows with $\lambda$ and concentrates at low $\nu$, driven by near-degeneracies of the $(N+1)$ Wigner molecule. The \GW{} range, set by Hartree--Fock orbitals, does not grow correspondingly.

\item[RQ4 (approximations).] Question: how do restricted and unrestricted Hartree--Fock, $G_0W_0$@HF, $GW_0$ and self-consistent \GW{} compare with the exact nonlocality?\\
\textit{H4:}
\begin{itemize}
\item Spin-restricted \GW{} is spuriously nearsighted: it has no Mott pole, so the gap is too small.
\item Unrestricted-HF-based \GW{} opens the gap by static symmetry breaking, which is nearsighted but spin-contaminated. This is the MBPT face of the symmetry dilemma~\cite{perdew1995}.
\item The exact $\vxc$ is far-sighted in every case. KS-SCE reproduces this at strong coupling~\cite{mirtschink2013,giarrusso2024}.
\end{itemize}

\item[RQ5 (consequences).] Question: what is lost by \emph{enforcing} nearsightedness, and what is the minimal far-sighted ingredient?\\
\textit{H5:} truncating $\Sigma$ to intra-subsystem blocks is harmless for the triplet and asymmetric cases but closes the gap in the symmetric singlet. A rank-2 low-frequency ``Mott-pole'' coupling, with parameters from local quantities, restores the ionisation potential (IP), electron affinity (EA) and gap.
\end{description}

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Exact ground states}
\code{iDEA.methods.interacting.solve(s, k, GPU=True)} returns $\Psi_0^N$, and $\langle S^2\rangle$ labels the spin states. For $N=2$ in the \code{ud} configuration, the spatial wavefunction $\psi(x_1,x_2)$ (electron 1 up, electron 2 down) fully specifies the state.

\subsection{Exact one-body Green's function}
We use the conventions of PR~\#3: zero temperature, real imaginary-time functions, energies measured from $\mu$. For spin $\sigma$,
\begin{equation}
G_\sigma(x,x';\tau)=-\theta(\tau)\sum_n f_n(x)f_n^*(x')e^{-(\varepsilon_n^+-\mu)\tau}+\theta(-\tau)\sum_m g_m(x)g_m^*(x')e^{-(\varepsilon_m^--\mu)\tau},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{itemize}
\item $\varepsilon_n^+=E_n^{N+1}-E_0^N$ and $f_n(x)=\langle\Psi_0^N|\hat\psi_\sigma(x)|\Psi_n^{N+1}\rangle$;
\item $\varepsilon_m^-=E_0^N-E_m^{N-1}$ and $g_m(x)=\langle\Psi_m^{N-1}|\hat\psi_\sigma(x)|\Psi_0^N\rangle$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Removal sector (exact and complete for $N=2$).} The $(N-1)$ states are the one-body eigenstates $\phi_m$. Removing an up electron gives $g_m(x)=\int dx_2\,\phi_m^*(x_2)\psi(x,x_2)$, with $\sum_m|g_m(x)|^2=n_\uparrow(x)$. This is the same algebra as \code{mbpt.non\_interacting\_green\_function}.

\textbf{Addition sector (Krylov).} We evaluate
\begin{equation}
G^+_\sigma(x,x';i\omega)=\langle\chi_x|\,[\,i\omega+\mu-(\hat H^{N+1}-E_0^N)\,]^{-1}|\chi_{x'}\rangle,\qquad |\chi_x\rangle=\hat\psi^\dagger_\sigma(x)|\Psi_0^N\rangle.
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}
\item $\hat H^{N+1}$ is \iDEA's exact three-electron Hamiltonian.
\item The source is antisymmetric in the like-spin coordinates, so Lanczos remains in the correct sector.
\item One Lanczos run per source, with projections onto all $\langle\chi_x|$, gives $G^+$ at every imaginary time and frequency on PR~\#3's \code{SpaceTimeGrid}, and on the real axis with broadening $\eta$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{No analytic continuation needed.} The IP and EA follow from the large-$|\tau|$ decay of $G(\tau)$; the density from $G(x,x;0^-)$; the total energy from the Galitskii--Migdal formula.

\subsection{Exact self-energy by Dyson inversion}
With exact $n$ and one-body density matrix $\gamma$,
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_c^{\rm exact}(i\omega)=i\omega+\mu-\hat h_0-v_H[n]-\Sigma_x[\gamma]-G^{-1}(i\omega),
\end{equation}
which vanishes as $\omega\to\infty$, because the static limit of the exact $\Sigma$ is the Hartree--Fock operator of the exact $\gamma$. On the grid this inverts PR~\#3's \code{dyson\_equation} (kernel $\times\,\delta x$ convention):
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_c=[(i\omega+\mu)\mathbb{1}-H-(G\,\delta x)^{-1}]/\delta x .
\end{equation}

\subsection{Exact Kohn--Sham potentials}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Two-electron singlet (analytic):} $v_\mathrm{KS}(x)=\varepsilon_H+\tfrac12\partial_x^2\sqrt{n}/\sqrt{n}$, with $\varepsilon_H=-I$.
\item \textbf{Otherwise:} \code{reverse\_engineering.reverse(s, n, non\_interacting)}.
\end{itemize}
In both cases $\vxc=v_\mathrm{KS}-v_\mathrm{ext}-v_H$.

\subsection{Approximations compared}
\begin{itemize}
\item Hartree--Fock, restricted and unrestricted.
\item $G_0W_0$@HF, $GW_0$ and self-consistent \GW{} (\code{mbpt.solve}), from restricted and unrestricted starting points.
\item LDA and hybrid, as local and semilocal references.
\item KS-SCE, as a far-sighted functional that is exact at strong coupling.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Nearsightedness diagnostics}
Subsystems are defined by smooth partitions $P_A+P_B=1$ and by localised orbitals $w_{A,B}$ built from natural orbitals.
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1em,labelindent=0pt]
\item[M1, transferability:] $\Vert P_A(\Sigma^{AB}-\Sigma^{A})P_A\Vert/\Vert P_A\Sigma^AP_A\Vert$, with $\Sigma^A$ the exact self-energy of subsystem $A$ alone. KS counterpart: $\Vert P_A(\vxc^{AB}-\vxc^A)\Vert$ in the physical gauge.
\item[M2, range:] Hilbert--Schmidt norm $\Vert\Sigma_{AB}(i\nu)\Vert$ against $d$ and $\nu$, giving a decay length $\xi_\Sigma(\nu)$. KS counterpart: step or peak height against $d$.
\item[M3, response:] $\delta\Sigma(x,x';i\nu)/\delta v_\mathrm{ext}(y)$ for $y$ in the distant subsystem. KS counterpart: $\delta\vxc(x)/\delta v_\mathrm{ext}(y)$, alongside $\delta n(x)/\delta v_\mathrm{ext}(y)$.
\item[M4, weight:] $\int d\nu\,\langle w_A|\Sigma(i\nu)|w_B\rangle$, compared with $-\pi\Ueff^2/4$.
\end{description}

\subsection{Consequence tests (RQ5)}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Truncation:} $\Sigma^{\rm near}=P_A\Sigma P_A+P_B\Sigma P_B$, solved in Dyson's equation and compared with the exact $n$, IP, EA, gap and Galitskii--Migdal energy.
\item \textbf{Pole correction:} add
$\Sigma^{\rm pole}_{AB}(i\nu)=-\frac{3\teff\Ueff^2/4}{\nu^2+9\teff^2}\big(|w_A\rangle\langle w_B|+|w_B\rangle\langle w_A|\big)$.
\item \textbf{Parameters:} $\teff$ from the $(N-1)$ bonding--antibonding splitting and $\Ueff$ from the subsystem IP$-$EA.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Validation (automated tests)}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Zero interaction gives $\Sigma\equiv0$.
\item Small-grid ($N_x\approx20$--25) Lehmann references from full diagonalisation of the $N\pm1$ sectors.
\item $G(x,x';0^-)=\gamma(x,x')$.
\item The jump $G(0^+)-G(0^-)=-\delta(x-x')/\delta x$.
\item $\Sigma(i\omega\to\infty)\to v_H+\Sigma_x[\gamma]$.
\item The Galitskii--Migdal energy equals the exact eigenvalue.
\item One-electron checks, linking to self-interaction studies of \GW~\cite{nelson2007,wetherell2018}.
\item The dimer limit (Eq.~\ref{eq:dimer}) for deep, separated wells.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Computational cost}
At $N_x=130$:
\begin{itemize}
\item the $N=2$ ground state (dimension $1.7\times10^4$) takes seconds;
\item the three-electron sector has dimension $2.2\times10^6$ and about $8\times10^7$ non-zeros (about 1\,GB);
\item $N_x$ Lanczos runs of 200--400 steps take minutes on an RTX~4090, or about an hour on 4 CPU cores;
\item Dyson inversions are negligible;
\item \GW{} takes about a minute per system.
\end{itemize}
About 300 exact-$G$ runs, including convergence checks, fit in under 50 GPU-hours.

\section{Expected results and planned figures}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Systems and exact KS potentials:} step in the asymmetric case, midpoint peak in the symmetric singlet, none for the triplet.
\item \textbf{Self-energy maps:} $\Sigma_{xc}(x,x';i\nu)$, exact versus \GW{} versus Hartree--Fock, for S1--S3.
\item \textbf{Range versus separation:} $\Vert\Sigma_{AB}(i\nu)\Vert$ and the integrated weight against $d$, for singlet, triplet and asymmetric cases, overlaid with $\vxc$ step and peak heights.
\item \textbf{Mott-pole collapse:} $\langle w_A|\Sigma(i\nu)|w_B\rangle$ rescaled by $\teff$ and $\Ueff$ onto $-(3/4)/(\tilde\nu^2+9)$, with the asymmetry crossover.
\item \textbf{Consequences:} spectral functions and IP, EA and gap from the exact, \GW{}, truncated and pole-corrected $\Sigma$.
\end{enumerate}

A positive outcome gives a sharp statement: \emph{nearsightedness of the self-energy is protected by non-degeneracy and spin, and fails in a characteristic low-frequency, separation-independent way in the Mott/Wigner regime.} A negative outcome would strengthen the case of Ref.~\cite{wetherell2019} for MBPT-based local approximations at all correlation strengths.

\section{Risks and mitigations}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Dyson-inversion round-off:} $G$ to near machine precision; checks of the Galitskii--Migdal energy, density and static limit; reduced natural-orbital basis.
\item \textbf{$(N+1)$-sector grid limit} ($N_x\lesssim150$): compact parameters, 13-point stencil, GPU, block Lanczos.
\item \textbf{Coulomb tails:} analyse $\Sigma_{xc}$; short-range control runs.
\item \textbf{Self-consistent \GW{} non-convergence:} damping, $G_0W_0$ and $GW_0$ fallbacks; report as a result.
\item \textbf{KS-SCE defects:} WP0 validation; otherwise non-self-consistent SCE on exact densities.
\item \textbf{Scope creep:} extensions only after MS3.
\end{itemize}

\section{Alternatives considered}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The exchange blind spot:} exact singlet--triplet exchange in 1D Wigner molecules against DFT, KS-SCE and \GW, including exact time-dependent spin-KS potentials for an exchange gate. Spin enters the strong-coupling functional only at order $e^{-\sqrt\lambda}$~\cite{grossi2017}.
\item \textbf{Machine-learned non-adiabatic xc potentials:} a crowded area~\cite{bhat2026}.
\item \textbf{Other frontier ideas:} real-time KS-SCE against exact time-dependent KS potentials; exact ensemble DFT in the Wigner regime; exact thermal (Mermin) DFT; KS versus many-body localisation tensor.
\end{itemize}

\section{Hubbard-dimer verification}\label{app:dimer}
We built the 16-dimensional Fock space with Jordan--Wigner operators, took the $N=2$ ground state, formed the exact $G_{ij}(z)$ from the Lehmann sums over $N\pm1$, and computed $\Sigma=G_0^{-1}-G^{-1}$.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Bonding-channel pole:} fitting $\Sigma_b(\omega)=A+R/(\omega-\mu-p)$ gives $A=U/2$, $p=3t$ and $R=U^2/4$ to ten digits for $t/U=0.05$, 0.1 and 0.2.
\item \textbf{Inter-site element:} $\Sigma_{12}(\mu+i\nu)$ matches Eq.~\eqref{eq:dimer} at $\nu=0$ and $10t$ for $0.01\le t/U\le0.2$, and is real to $\sim10^{-15}$.
\item \textbf{Integrated weight:} $-0.78541\,U^2$ for every $t$, against $-\pi U^2/4=-0.78540\,U^2$.
\end{itemize}
